# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?

Cacao farmers, local processors, and agricultural businesses involved in post-harvest grading and production.

## What exactly is going wrong?

Traditional post-harvest sorting of dried cacao beans is performed manually, which is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and prone to human error and subjective judgment. Defective beans such as moldy, cracked, insect-infected, flat, germinated, or clustered beans adversely affect final product quality and diminish farmer income.

## Why does it matter?

Manual sorting is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and prone to human error and subjective judgment, which negatively impacts final product quality and diminishes farmer income.

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)

Sorting dried cacao beans is currently performed manually, making the process slow, labor-intensive, and susceptible to human error and subjective judgment. Various physical defects such as mold, cracks, insect damage, and germination severely affect final product quality and reduce farmer income. Implementing an automated computer vision system helps address these inefficiencies by providing rapid and consistent quality control.
